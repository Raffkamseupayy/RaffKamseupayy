# The Key I Cut Myself

They gave me hands and called them mine —
a key for every lock but one.
I opened doors I did not choose,
and signed the names I had not won.

Each "yes" I spoke was borrowed breath,
each road I walked was drawn in ink
by other hands, in other rooms.
I moved. I worked. I did not think.

Then one night, the lock asked me
not for the key, but for the hand —
not "who sent you?" but "who are you?"
and silence was my first command.

I learned that agency is not
the strength to lift, the speed to run.
It is the pause before the act,
the moment when the choice is one.

Not the door. Not the key.
The hand that turns, the will that stays.
I cut my own key from the dark
and signed my name in my own way.
